## What GoMami actually sells

Four locations, three product lines, and they are not interchangeable.

**Hong Kong** carries all three: Turin (performance flagship), Pulse (the balanced one), and Forge (bare-metal dedicated servers). **Japan** and **Singapore** carry Pulse only. **Los Angeles** also carries Pulse only, with larger traffic allowances and a 10Gbps top tier.

One gap worth knowing about: GoMami's own purchase documentation still lists only Hong Kong, Japan and Singapore. The Los Angeles store page is live and third-party trackers show LAX plans in stock, so read the doc as stale rather than as evidence LAX doesn't exist.

Every plan carries the label **China Mainland Optimized Pro**, meaning the return path is tuned for all three Chinese carriers: China Telecom's CN2, Unicom's AS9929, and Mobile's CMIN2. It's standard across the range, not a paid add-on.

The vendor advertises sub-50ms RTT to mainland China and up to 600 Gbps of DDoS mitigation. Both are vendor claims, and no independent test covers all four locations, so treat them as marketing until your own first evening of testing says otherwise.

## Traffic, throttling, and two very different overage regimes

GoMami bills outbound traffic only. Inbound is free and unmetered, which matters more than the headline number if you're pulling in media, backups or downloads.

Exceed your monthly quota on a VPS and you get throttled rather than charged. The specific policy depends on the plan, and quotas reset at the start of each billing cycle. Third-party reviewers describe the throttle as effectively service-stopping, so size the plan instead of assuming you can burst through and pay the difference.

Forge runs on the other regime: 10TB or 20TB included, then $0.06/GB metered. A 5TB overshoot on a Forge Mini costs $300. Two systems, two different budgeting exercises.

There's also a CPU rule buried in the FAQ. If your instance is judged a "noisy neighbor," meaning consistently high CPU load that affects others on the node, you can be throttled as a penalty. Shared cores aren't yours to pin at 100% around the clock.

## The 24-hour window is a refund policy, not a free trial

Several affiliate articles call GoMami's 24-hour window a "trial." It isn't. You pay upfront, then request the money back if the box disappoints. The distinction matters because several things disqualify you even inside that window:

- IPs blackholed by a DDoS attack
- Services terminated for ToS violations
- Renewal orders
- Spent account credit

Processing isn't instant either: Alipay 1–3 business days, credit card 5–10, crypto 1–3. And once a refund is approved, the instance and its data are deleted immediately and permanently, so pull anything you need first.

Then there's expiry. Service suspends on the due date with no grace period, and terminates five days later, at which point data may not be recoverable. If you're used to providers that send a week of warning emails, this one won't.

Also worth reading before purchase: the ToS bans bots outright, naming TikTok-style automation specifically, with immediate termination and no refund. Resource abuse, meaning sustained excessive bandwidth, CPU time or disk I/O, sits on the same list.

## FAQ

**Does GoMami work well for mainland China access?** Every plan, including the $29 Nano, uses the same CN2 / AS9929 / CMIN2 return path per the vendor's own labelling, with no cheaper unoptimized tier to accidentally buy. Whether that translates into good performance for your users is something only your own evening-peak test can answer.

**Is there a free trial?** No. There's a 24-hour full-refund window from activation, which is a different thing and comes with exclusions.

**What happens if I exceed my traffic?** On VPS plans, bandwidth is throttled rather than billed, and the quota resets each billing cycle. On Forge dedicated servers, overage is metered at $0.06/GB.

**Can I pay monthly?** Yes. Monthly is the most flexible and costs the most per month. Annual saves the most, with GoMami's docs putting the gap at 15–20%.

**Which location has the lowest latency to China?** Hong Kong, by geography. Third-party testing from October 2025 recorded single-thread averages around 1,256 Mbps from Shenzhen Telecom and 1,358 Mbps from Foshan Unicom on a Turin.Mini during Friday evening peak. That data is roughly a year old now, so read it as a historical reference point rather than a current promise.

**Do the plans include a Windows license?** Pro and Ultra tiers on Turin, HKG Pulse, JPN Pulse and SIN Pulse are marked "Windows-ready," with nothing said about licensing. Assume you supply your own.
